Chemistry of FU Orionis Objects

Unveiling the molecular complexity of young stellar outbursts

Infrared Spectroscopy Young Stellar Objects (YSOs) Accretion Outbursts Chemistry

Project Overview

FU Orionis objects are young stars that experience dramatic increases in brightness due to sudden episodes of high-mass accretion from their surrounding disks. This research focuses on investigating the chemical composition of these objects during these violent events.

By analyzing infrared observations, we can trace how these outbursts impact the molecular gas and dust in the inner regions of planet-forming disks, altering the environment where future planetary systems will emerge.

Key Objectives

  • Identify molecular species (CO, H2O, OH) in the circumstellar environment of FUors.
  • Characterize the physical conditions (temperature and density) of the gas during accretion outbursts.
  • Compare chemical signatures across different types of young stellar outbursts to understand their evolution.
  • Understand the impact of high luminosity events on the chemical survival of molecules.

Methodology

We utilize high-resolution Infrared Spectroscopy data from ground-based and space telescopes. Through radiative transfer modeling and spectral analysis, we extract information about the excitation temperatures and column densities of the detected molecules.
