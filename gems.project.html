Gaseous Planets around M-dwarf stars (GEMS)

TESS Data Transit Photometry M-Dwarfs

The GEMS project aims to address one of the most intriguing questions in modern exoplanet science: How do gas giant planets form around low-mass stars? Historically, core accretion models predicted that M-dwarfs shouldn't frequently host Jovian planets, yet surveys like GEMS are finding fascinating exceptions.

Figure 1: Example of a transit light curve from a candidate in the GEMS survey.

Research Goals

  • Detection & Confirmation: Identifying new gas giants using TESS photometry and confirming them via ground-based facilities like SPECULOOS and LCOGT.
  • Statistical Census: Building a robust sample to determine the occurrence rate of giants around small stars.
  • Evolutionary Tracks: Studying the migration patterns and atmospheric potential of these systems.
Key Methodology: We utilize high-precision radial velocity data from instruments like HARPS to break the mass-radius degeneracy and confirm the planetary nature of our candidates.
